Projects
  • RiverCam AI: Computer Vision Flood Monitoring

    RiverCam AI explores the use of computer vision and environmental sensors to monitor stream conditions and improve flood detection. The project combines camera imagery, ultrasonic water-level measurements, and field observations to investigate methods for estimating stream discharge and identifying changing river conditions. The long-term goal is to develop accessible, automated monitoring techniques that support flood research and early-warning systems.

  • AppAqua: Machine Learning for Water Quality Data

    AppAqua is an interdisciplinary research initiative at Appalachian State University studying water quality in the Upper South Fork of the New River watershed. This project develops machine learning methods to reconstruct missing observations in long-term environmental datasets containing millions of measurements. Researchers investigate statistical and deep learning approaches for estimating water temperature, depth, conductivity, and pH, incorporating seasonal patterns and external hydrological and meteorological data.

  • AppMAIS: AI and Bee Research

    AppMAIS highlights interdisciplinary research at Appalachian State University exploring applications of artificial intelligence to bee-related research. The project connects machine learning and biological research, demonstrating how computational methods can support the analysis of complex biological data. It provides an example of how AI can contribute to scientific questions beyond traditional computer science applications.

  • AI for Glaucoma Research

    Glaucoma research at Appalachian State University explores the intersection of artificial intelligence and medical research. Machine learning offers methods for analyzing complex datasets, identifying patterns, and supporting research into diseases affecting vision. This research area demonstrates the potential for computational techniques to contribute to healthcare and biomedical discovery.

    Weather-Aware Robotic Guide Dog

    This robotics project investigates autonomous navigation using a simulated Unitree Go2 quadruped robot. Developed with ROS 2 and Gazebo, the system integrates weather information, environmental predictions, and autonomous navigation to make mission decisions. The robot is designed to navigate between waypoints, evaluate weather conditions, and return to its starting location when necessary. The project explores technologies relevant to future assistive robotics, including robotic guide dogs.
